# Lydia Chen-Walsh

Student in the Tuesday class in spring and autumn 2026. A nurse, writing a memoir about her working life.

## In Class

Lydia came to the voice term to find a narrating voice that was neither clinical nor confessional, and found one, in her words, "about halfway between" (quoted with her agreement). In the revision term she is cutting the memoir from about 90,000 words towards 70,000 and restructuring it by place rather than by year.

She writes very well about work and is careful about the people in it. Her questions raised the point I had not thought through: how to give useful feedback on a memoir that involves real people, some of them patients, without either the class or me becoming custodians of their stories. That is now [[how-do-i-give-feedback-on-memoir-about-real-people]], and the class rule that came out of it is in [[student-writing-privacy-rules]].

## How We Work Together

She works shifts, so she misses about one session in four and catches up from the handouts. I send nothing to her directly; handouts go through the centre's system ([[carrowmede-learner-data-policy]]). Nothing about her own circumstances belongs in this vault and none is recorded.

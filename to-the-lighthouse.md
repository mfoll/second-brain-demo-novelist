# To the Lighthouse

## Summary

Virginia Woolf's 1927 novel about the Ramsay family and their summer visits to a house by the sea between 1910 and 1920. A long first part on one evening, a short middle section ("Time Passes") in which ten years, a war and several deaths go by in a few pages, and a final part in which the delayed trip to the lighthouse is made.

## Why It Matters

The middle section is the best lesson I know in moving time fast without a summary voice, and in reporting deaths in brackets so that their weight comes from the understatement. I reread it in June 2025 when the first draft had a two-year gap between chapters that I was bridging with clumsy summary. It is also behind my doubt about the storm prologue: Woolf withholds the event, she does not open on it. Linked from [[modernist-women-writers]] and the craft notes on [[free-indirect-style]].

## Key Details

- Published 1927, the same year as the Saltmarrow storm in the novel, which is a coincidence I enjoy and will not mention in public.
- Three parts: "The Window", "Time Passes", "The Lighthouse".
- The sea is the constant backdrop; the lighthouse trip is the frame.

## Caveats

The article places the novel in a real location; the novel's setting has no bearing on Saltmarrow, and I borrow technique only.

# How Would Saltmarrow Hear Of The Wall Street Crash?

Part three of the third draft ends in late 1929. The draft 2 version had Nell hear of the crash "the same evening" in October 1929, which the [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]] flagged on 2026-07-30. How, and how fast, would news of it reach a small herring town, and would anyone there think it mattered?

## What I know

- Background reading on the crash and the period is general; it is not about any town like Saltmarrow.
- The telegraph office takes prices and shipping on the wire ([[electrical-telegraph]]); a curing firm with export customers might hear of a market fall by wire before the newspapers arrive.
- The Institute has had a wireless set since 1926 ([[should-saltmarrow-have-a-wireless-set-by-1927]]), so a news bulletin is possible.

## Options

1. Nell takes a wire for Hugh Rendell from his agent about cancelled orders. Plausible, quiet, and on her ground.
2. Someone hears it on the Institute wireless. Easy, but it makes the crash an announcement rather than a consequence.
3. Nobody notices for weeks, and the town learns it through falling herring prices in 1930. Truest, but after the book ends.

## Leaning

Option 1, with a hint of option 3 in Constance's last letter. The point for the book is not the crash but that the town's money was already going. Decide before writing chapter 30 ([[third-draft-chapter-map]]).

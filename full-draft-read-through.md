# Full Draft Read-Through

How I read a complete draft before anyone else sees it. The rule came out of draft 2: I reached most of Imogen's July conclusions on my own late read in June, which I should have done before sending ([[draft-two-review-2026-07-08]]).

## Inputs

- A complete draft compiled to paper: single-sided, 1.5 spacing, wide right margin.
- A pencil, a red pen, and a blank notebook page per chapter.
- At least five clear days. Not interleaved with teaching weeks where possible.

## Steps

1. **Wait.** At least seven days between finishing and reading. Do something else: research, class prep, a different book.
2. **First pass: as a reader.** Read the whole draft in no more than three sittings, in order, without marking anything except a pencil tick where attention dropped and a cross where I was confused about where or when I was. No line edits.
3. **Write the reader's report** to myself, one page, before opening the notebook: what the book is about now, where it dragged, what I wanted more of, which character I would follow out of the room.
4. **Second pass: as an editor.** Chapter by chapter with the red pen. For each chapter, one line on its job in the book; if I cannot write it, the chapter is in doubt.
5. **Continuity pass.** Dates, ages, the boat, the weather, the hymn titles, against [[character-bible]], [[saltmarrow-town-bible]] and [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]].
6. **Fix list.** Sort into structural, scene and line. Do structural fixes before sending, or note them in the cover letter if they need the editor's view.

## Verification

The reader's report from step 3 should agree with what Teodora says when she reads. If it does not, I was reading as the author.

# Royalty Payment

English Wikipedia article, last edited 2026-10-02, read on 2026-10-05 while reconciling the Lantern Hours statement that showed the earn-out.

## Summary

A royalty is a payment from one party to the owner of an asset for the continuing right to use it. Royalties are usually set as a percentage of gross or net revenue from that use, or as a fixed sum per unit sold; other bases exist. A royalty interest is the right to a stream of future royalty payments.

## Why It Matters

The distinction between a percentage of gross and a percentage of net is the whole of the clause that surprised me in April ([[lantern-hours-royalty-statement-2026-04]]): high-discount sales pay 10% of net receipts rather than 10% of the retail price. Reading the general definition helped me explain the statement lines in [[reconcile-a-royalty-statement]] without leaning on publishing jargon. Read alongside [[advance-against-royalties]].

## Caveats

The article covers royalties across industries (music, patents, natural resources); nothing in it is specific to book contracts, reserves or agency commission.

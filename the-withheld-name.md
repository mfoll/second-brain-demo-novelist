# The Withheld Name

Leaving a character unnamed for a stretch of a book so that the reader attends to what they do rather than who they are. The name, when it arrives, recasts what came before.

## How I use it

- The [[storm-prologue]] names nobody: a coxswain, a bowman, the men at the oars. The reader cannot yet attach the loss to a face, which is the point of the prologue and my answer to Callum's objection that it gives the ending away.
- In translation I met it as a problem more often than a device: French and Portuguese can hold a pronoun back much longer than English will tolerate.

## Limits

Two or three pages at most before it reads as coyness. Never in a letter: Constance would always name people.

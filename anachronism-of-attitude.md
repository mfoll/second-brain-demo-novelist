# Anachronism Of Attitude

An anachronism of thought rather than of object: a character in 1925 who thinks about trauma, women's work, religion or class in a vocabulary and with assumptions from the 2020s. Harder to catch than a wristwatch, because it usually feels like sympathy.

## Examples caught

- Tobias reflecting on "processing" his war. Gone; he does not reflect on it at all, he avoids it.
- Nell thinking the gutting work "exploitative". She thinks it is hard, badly paid and hers.
- Constance's letters treating the chapel as quaint. She takes it seriously even when she mocks it.

## How to catch it

- Read the interior passages for abstract nouns. Period minds in my sources reach for concrete words and for scripture.
- Ask what the character would be ashamed of, not what I would be.
- Have a period reader. [[femi-adewale]] caught most of the Tobias cases.

## Origin

Notebook six, 2021, on translating a 1930s narrator whose casual prejudice an editor wanted softened. Softening it made the book lie about its time. The [[anachronism]] background article covers object and language anachronism well and attitude barely at all.

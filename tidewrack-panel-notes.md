# Tidewrack Panel Notes

My preparation for the [[tidewrack-festival-panel-2026-06-20|panel of 20 June]], written against [[tidewrack-panel-brief-2026|Kwabena's questions]].

## Opening (aim: 8 minutes; took 12)

- Twenty-two years of translating is twenty-two years of listening to someone else's sentences until you can hear where they breathe.
- A historical novel asks the same of a period. You are not decorating, you are carrying.

## Points to make

- Free indirect style is where translation and fiction meet: the narration borrows a voice without quotation marks ([[free-indirect-style]]).
- Archaism lies. People in 1925 did not think they sounded old. Use their vocabulary, not a costume.
- A translator hides choices; a novelist has to make them visible enough to be trusted.
- The translator's name belongs on the cover.

## Passage (under a minute)

The dictionary scene, the first eight lines only.

## Not to say

Anything about book two beyond "the 1920s, a coastal town, and a choir". Agreed with Priyanka.

## After

The poet's point that dialect is "only local" was the best thing said. I want it for the class on voice.

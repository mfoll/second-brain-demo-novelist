# Residencies Are For Finishing, Not Starting

## Stance

A residency is best used to finish a defined piece of work already under way, not to start something new or to "think". Two weeks away from ordinary life magnify whatever you bring: momentum or drift.

## Scope

Short residencies of one to four weeks, for a novelist with a draft. Longer residencies or residencies built around a theme ([[artist-in-residence]] covers the range) may work differently; I have no experience of them.

## Why I Think This

- Ferrow House, August 2026: one goal (part one of the third draft), 21,000 words in eleven working days, goal met ([[ferrow-house-residency-2026-08-10]]).
- Two colleagues' accounts of residencies spent starting new books, which both described as lovely and unproductive. Hearsay, but consistent.
- My own week at a translators' retreat in 2018, spent reading round a book I never translated.

## What Would Change My Mind

A residency that started a book which then got written. One case would do.

## Implications

- Apply only with a narrow, finishable goal ([[ferrow-house-residency-plan]] is the model).
- That is why [[should-i-go-back-to-ferrow-house-in-2027]] is deferred: in 2027 there will be nothing to finish until the copy-edit.

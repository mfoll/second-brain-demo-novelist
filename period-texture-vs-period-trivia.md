# Period Texture Versus Period Trivia

A test for any period detail in a draft. Texture is a detail a character would notice, use or suffer at that moment; trivia is a detail the author knows and wants the reader to know. Texture stays; trivia goes, however hard it was to find.

## The test

Ask three things of the detail:

1. Would this character notice it now, in this scene?
2. Does it change what the character does or feels?
3. Would a reader miss it if it were gone?

Two noes and it is trivia.

## Examples from The Salt Choir

- Texture: the gutters' cotton-bound fingers in chapter 1. Nell's hands hurt; she notices.
- Texture: forty minutes from maroon to afloat in the storm. Tobias counts them.
- Trivia, cut: the weight of a full herring barrel in chapter 6. Nell is at the telegraph counter by then and would not think of it.
- Trivia, cut: the date of the war memorial's unveiling. Nobody in 1927 thinks of the memorial as dated.

## Origin

From translation, notebook nine: a translator who footnotes every period object kills the book. The novel has to carry its period the way the original did, as things people use without explaining. Transcribed in [[craft-notebook-digitisation]].

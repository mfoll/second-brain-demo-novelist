# Maureen Tighe

Student in the Tuesday class since autumn 2025. Retired school librarian. The widest reader in the room by a distance.

## In Class

Maureen is writing a novel about a mobile library in the 1960s, in broad terms, and is on her second draft. She brings books to class: if someone is struggling with a voice, she will have a public-domain example in her bag the following week. She is generous in feedback and exact about it, and she will say when an exercise has not worked, which is useful.

## The Letter Exercise

On [[class-session-2026-01-27|27 January 2026]], in the session on epistolary voice, Maureen offered an exercise she had used with teenage readers at her library: write a letter from a character to the one person they do not perform for, and let the reader work out the relationship from the letter alone. I set it in place of my own exercise for the second half of the evening and wrote one myself, in the voice of Constance Leal writing to her sister ([[constance-sample-letter-2026-01-27]]).

That letter solved Constance's flat close-third chapters. From draft 2 onwards her chapters are letters ([[constance-leal]]), and they survived the partial reversal on 2026-07-08. In my notes Maureen's exercise is credited as the spark. I told her in February; she was pleased and slightly embarrassed. She has agreed to be thanked in the acknowledgements of The Salt Choir, and that I may reuse the exercise in later terms with her name on it ([[maureen-tighe-letter-exercise]]) and that I may mention the story in public in general terms. I have not quoted any of her own writing.

## How We Work Together

Comments in class and on paper. She lends me books and expects them back.

# How Do I Give Feedback On Memoir About Real People?

How should the class, and I, respond to memoir that involves identifiable real people, without becoming judges of other people's stories or of the writer's right to tell them?

## Why It Matters

[[lydia-chen-walsh]]'s memoir of her working life raised it in spring 2026, and the autumn revision term has two memoirists. The question will come back every term.

## Current Answer

Adopted for autumn 2026, and written into [[student-writing-privacy-rules]]:

- Feedback is on the page as writing: scene, voice, structure. Not on whether something should have been told.
- The writer decides what to share in class and can bring a version with details changed. Nobody asks who someone "really" is.
- What is read in the room stays in the room; I make no notes on memoir content at all, only on craft points.
- Consent and legal questions are the writer's, and I point to the centre's general guidance rather than giving advice.

## Still Uncertain

Whether a group exercise ever belongs on memoir. In autumn 2026 I give memoirists the option of an alternative exercise in every session. Revisit after the term.

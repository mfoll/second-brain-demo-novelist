# Storm Prologue

The four-page prologue that opens the third draft of The Salt Choir: the lifeboat launching into the November 1927 storm. All that remains of draft 2's storm-first opening. Still disputed.

## What it is

About 1,100 words, unnumbered, before part one. One night, one place: the slipway and the harbour mouth. The narration is outside every character, the only passage in the book that is ([[narrative-distance]]). It shows the maroons, the launchers on the ropes, the women on the harbour wall, a choir hymn half heard from the chapel where a rehearsal has stopped, and the boat clearing the harbour mouth under oars. It stops there.

## Rules it follows

Agreed with [[imogen-lacey|Imogen]] at the [[draft-two-review-2026-07-08|July review]]:

- No names. Not the coxswain, not the crew, not the vessel on the bar.
- No outcome. The reader does not see the boat return or not.
- No more than four pages.
- No viewpoint character's thoughts.

## Versions

Kept in [[storm-chapter-versions]]:

1. 2026-07-15: 2,400 words, cut from draft 2's chapters 1 and 2. Named Tobias. Too long.
2. 2026-07-22: 1,500 words, no names, ended with the boat lost to sight. Imogen: still too much.
3. 2026-08-21, the last morning at Ferrow House: 1,100 words, ends as the boat clears the harbour mouth. Current.

## The argument

[[callum-pryce|Callum]] says any storm prologue tells the reader the boat will be lost, and turns parts one and two into waiting ([[callum-email-on-prologue-2026-07-10]], [[callum-prologue-call-2026-09-17]]). Imogen and I say it asks a question rather than answering one, and gives the book its pull ([[a-prologue-must-earn-its-pages]], [[suspense-versus-surprise]]). Decision deferred until first readers have read parts one and two: [[should-the-salt-choir-keep-the-storm-prologue]].

One line from the current version, my draft: "Nobody on the wall said the word. They held the ropes and watched the oars."

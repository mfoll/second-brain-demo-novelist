# Nell Garrick

Viewpoint character of The Salt Choir, and the only one in all three drafts. A character of the novel; ages, household and family are fixed in [[character-bible]].

## Who she is

- Nineteen when the book opens in March 1924. Has gutted herring on the curing yards since she was fourteen, following the season with the crews.
- Leaves the yards for a clerk's place at the Saltmarrow telegraph office, which she got by being quicker at sums than the postmaster's nephew.
- Sharp, funny, impatient with the town and fiercely part of it. Reads anything. Sings alto in [[constance-leal|Constance]]'s choir from 1925, badly at first.

## Her role in each draft

- **Draft 1**: the only viewpoint, and overloaded. Wire messages were her way into everything, which was plausible for weather and prices and not for the lifeboat crew's quarrels. [[wendy-farrant]]'s "listening at keyholes" note was about her.
- **Draft 2**: one strand of three. Lighter, better. She opened the book on the storm night at the telegraph desk.
- **Draft 3**: close third, chapters alternating with Tobias. She sees what a telegraph clerk could see: weather, shipping and prices on the wire, the town at the counter, the gutting crews she has left ([[what-can-nell-know-from-the-telegraph-office]]). She takes the storm warnings on the night and is at the desk when the coastguard messages come in (chapter 22).

## Voice

Close third with heavy [[free-indirect-style]]. Short sentences when she is working, long ones when she is angry. Her idiom is the town's, half mocked. No dialect spelling.

## Arc in the third draft

From escaping the yards (part one) to holding the town's news on the worst night (part two) to deciding whether to leave Saltmarrow when the herring fails and the telegraph office is cut back (part three, 1929). Whether she goes is the question of the last part, and is not yet settled in the draft.

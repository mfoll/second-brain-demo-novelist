# Merrin Archive Visit, 12 May 2026

A reading-room day at the [[merrin-maritime-collection]] with [[eluned-parry]], booked a fortnight earlier to check the lifeboat in the storm chapters of draft 2, which had an engine. Ten items requested from the station records finding aid ([[merrin-lifeboat-station-records-finding-aid]]); eight produced, two as copies.

## Why I went

Draft 2 had Saltmarrow's lifeboat start its engine on the storm night. [[ronan-maddox]] had asked who started the engine after the [[spring-class-final-reading-2026-03-31|spring reading of 31 March]], and I had a bad feeling about it but no evidence. Draft 2 was due to Imogen on 2026-06-12, so this was the last chance to fix it before she read it.

## What the records showed

- The service record books of three small stations, 1924 to 1931. All three launched pulling and sailing boats throughout the late 1920s. One received a motor boat in 1929, one in 1932, the third not in the period I saw.
- Crew lists: thirteen names for a ten-oar boat was ordinary, with the coxswain, second coxswain and bowman named first.
- Launch returns describing launches by hand and by horse team down a beach, with the time from maroon to afloat often over half an hour in bad weather.
- A district inspector's letter recommending a motor boat for one station "when funds permit", with a pencilled note about a slipway that would have to be rebuilt first.

Eluned was careful to say that three stations are three stations, and that practice varied with money, beach and the inspector.

## Decisions

- No engine. The *Providence* is a ten-oar pulling and sailing boat. Recorded two days later as the 2026-05-14 entry in the [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]] and in the answered Question [[would-saltmarrows-lifeboat-have-had-an-engine-in-1927]] ([[lifeboat-question-answered-2026-05-14]]).
- The inspector's letter becomes the district committee's promise of a motor boat for 1928, a thread in part three.
- Launch time from maroon to afloat goes up to forty minutes in the storm chapters.

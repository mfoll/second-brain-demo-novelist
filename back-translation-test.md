# Back-Translation Test

My private check on a sentence or a voice: translate it in my head into French (sometimes Portuguese) and back, and see what survives.

## Why it works

Twenty-two years of translating taught me which parts of a sentence carry its meaning and which are only its English surface. Idiom, alliteration and fashionable words do not survive the trip. Thought, image, rhythm of clauses and the attitude of the speaker usually do. If a character's line still sounds like her after French and back, the voice is in what she thinks, not just in how she says it.

## What it catches

- Voices made of dialect markers and nothing else. Draft-one Nell failed it badly: take out "aye" and "down the harbour" and she sounded like me.
- Period costume: a 1925 phrase that carries no thought ([[period-diction]]).
- Sentences that are clever in English only. Some I keep anyway; I want to know which they are.

## What it does not catch

Sound. Some of Constance's best sentences fail the test because their point is their music in English. [[sunniva-brekke]] reading aloud is the check for those.

## Limits

It is a private habit, not a method I would teach as such. In class I teach a weaker version: say the line in plain words, then decide what the character's version adds.

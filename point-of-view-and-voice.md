# Point Of View And Voice

Retrieval surface for whose eyes a chapter uses, how close the narration sits to them, and how each character sounds on the page. It overlaps with my teaching: the spring 2026 class was on exactly this ([[evening-class-spring-2026]]).

## How it touches my work

The Salt Choir went from one viewpoint to three. [[nell-garrick]] and [[tobias-arden]] are in close third person with a lot of [[free-indirect-style]]; [[constance-leal]] speaks only in letters to her sister ([[letter-chapters]]). Each needs a different distance and a different vocabulary, and each has to sound like 1925 without sounding like a costume ([[period-diction]]).

Twenty-two years of translating gave me one habit that matters here: I test a voice by imagining it in French. If the sentence survives the trip, the voice is in the thought, not just the idiom ([[back-translation-test]]).

## What I have learned so far

Voice problems are usually knowledge problems. Nell sounded false in draft one because she was reporting things she could not have known. Constance sounded flat in close third because her real voice was the one she used for her sister, not for herself. Moving her into letters fixed her in a single evening, at the [[class-session-2026-01-27|class of 27 January 2026]].

# Invented Towns Are More Honest Than Real Ones

## Stance

For a novel that invents a disaster, an invented town is the honest choice. It admits the fiction openly and lets me be accurate about the period without being inaccurate about a place.

## Scope

Historical fiction in which the central event is invented. A novel about a real event in a real place is a different contract.

## Why I Think This

- A real town has real families and a real lifeboat history. Inventing a loss there would sit on top of a real one, or invent one where there was none.
- With a real town, every reader who knows the street becomes a fact-checker of the wrong things.
- The research gets better, not worse: it has to establish what is plausible for a town of this size, which is the question that matters to the story. See [[would-saltmarrows-lifeboat-have-had-an-engine-in-1927]].

## What Would Change My Mind

Evidence that readers trust invented places less, in reviews of the published book. I will look in 2027.

## Implications

- Never name a real town, region or country in the novel or these notes.
- Real events stay in the background and never happen in Saltmarrow.
- The decision that settled the early question: [[should-saltmarrow-be-a-real-town]].

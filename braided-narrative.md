# Braided Narrative

A structure in which two or more strands, usually tied to different characters or times, are interleaved chapter by chapter and converge on a shared event. A kind of [[nonlinear-narrative]] when the strands sit in different years.

## What it promises

- Width: the town seen from several places at once.
- A built-in engine: each strand ends on a hook and the reader is carried across the gap.
- Convergence: the pleasure of watching separate lines meet.

## What it costs

Learned the hard way in [[braided-structure-rewrite]]:

- The reader must hold every strand's position in time. Three strands in different years, plus a frame, was one too many. Tobias's strand was the casualty.
- Each crossing spends a little of the reader's attention on orientation. Draft 2 needed about 6,000 more words than draft 1, mostly re-establishing where and when.
- If the convergence point is shown first, the strands become a countdown, not a discovery ([[suspense-versus-surprise]]).
- It flatters the planner. The braid was the best plan I ever made; the plan was not the book.

## When I would use it again

Two strands, not three; strands that share a time and differ in place; a convergence the reader does not yet know about. The third draft keeps a rotation of viewpoints, which is a weak braid inside one timeline ([[third-draft-chapter-map]]).

# What can Nell know from the telegraph office?

## Question

Nell's position as a telegraph clerk was meant to make her the town's ear. What would a clerk in a small office in the 1920s actually have seen and heard, so that her chapters stay plausible?

## Current Answer

Answered for the third draft, from the research in [[saltmarrow-research]]:

- Weather and gale warnings, herring prices and shipping movements, which come over the wire as routine.
- Private telegrams she sends and receives: births, deaths, money, travel. She is bound not to repeat them, which is a source of tension, not of plot convenience.
- Whatever people say at the counter.
- On the storm night, messages between the coastguard, the lifeboat station's honorary secretary and the district, which pass through the office.

She does not know what is said in the lifeboat house or the chapel vestry. Those scenes now belong to Tobias and Constance.

## Caveats

Whether the office had a telephone by 1927 is unchecked; raised at the October group. Goes to [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]].

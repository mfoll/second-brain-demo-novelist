# Shared Authority

An idea from oral history practice: an interview is made by two people, and the interviewee has a claim on how what they said is interpreted, not only on whether it is recorded. Ifeoma Clarke introduced it at the [[oral-history-workshop-2025-03-29|Merrin workshop]] as a principle oral historians argue about, not a rule.

## Why it unsettles a novelist

Fiction takes. A novelist absorbs what she hears into characters and changes it until the source cannot see it. Shared authority says the source should be able to see it, and to object.

## Where I have landed for now

- For the interview itself: Dorothy saw my summary, corrected it, and decided where the recording goes.
- For the novel: a middle position. Where her memory shaped a scene closely enough that she might recognise it, she sees the scene before the book is delivered. Everything else is texture. See [[how-much-of-dorothys-story-can-the-novel-use]].

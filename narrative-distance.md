# Narrative Distance

How close the narration sits to a character's mind at a given moment, from outside description ("A man stood on the slipway") through reported thought to full [[free-indirect-style]]. Also called psychic distance. It can change within a paragraph, and the change is itself meaning.

## How I use it

- **Nell** stays close almost all the time. When the narration pulls back from her it is to show what she cannot see.
- **Tobias**: distance is the main tool for the shell shock. On ordinary days the narration is close and calm. When a memory takes him, the narration steps back and describes his hands, the rope, the sea, as if from the slipway. The reader sees him go away from himself. [[femi-adewale]] said this read truer than any interior description of panic.
- **Constance** has no distance to vary: her letters are first person. Her equivalent is what she leaves out.

## Rule of thumb I teach

Move closer for feeling, further for consequence. And never change distance by accident: mark each shift with something on the page, a change of sentence length, a concrete object, a time cue.

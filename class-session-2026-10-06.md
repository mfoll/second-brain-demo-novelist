# Class Session 2026-10-06

Session 4 of the [[evening-class-autumn-2026|autumn 2026 revision term]]. Subject: the [[reverse-outline]]. Fourteen students present.

## Plan

- 19:00 to 19:20: what a reverse outline is, using [[reverse-outline-handout-2026-10-06]]. One line per scene: what happens, whose viewpoint, what changes.
- 19:20 to 20:00: everyone outlines their first 3,000 words in class.
- 20:15 to 21:00: what the outlines show.

## What Happened

- [[samir-benhaddou]] had already outlined his whole crime novel in a spreadsheet and found two clues paid off before they were planted. He showed the structure, not the text. The room took more from his columns than from my handout; with his agreement they are now an appendix to it.
- [[olu-bankole]] asked what to do when the outline shows that your best chapter belongs to a different book. Twenty minutes on that: keep a file of cut material and stop treating it as loss.
- Three students rebuilt their revision plans from their outlines by the following week.

## For Me

I reverse-outlined part one of the Salt Choir third draft the weekend before, to test the handout. It showed that Tobias's first chapter carries two scenes that Nell's next chapter repeats. I have not told the class that; my book is not a workshop text in this room.

## Change Next Time

Give the outline thirty minutes, not forty. Use the extra ten for Samir-style columns from the start.

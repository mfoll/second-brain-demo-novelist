# Interwar period

## Summary

The interwar period runs from the armistice of 11 November 1918 to 1 September 1939. The article describes a short era of large social, political and economic change: mechanisation and petroleum energy, the prosperity of the 1920s for parts of the middle class, cars, electric light and radio becoming common in wealthier countries, and then the Great Depression.

## Why It Matters

The frame of the whole novel, read in the second week of the research. The useful contrast is between the decade the article describes, of new machines and new money, and a herring town where almost none of it arrives: paraffin lamps in the curing yards, one wireless set in the Institute, a lifeboat still rowed. Saltmarrow lives at the edge of the period's story, which is the point of the book. The year-level background column of the [[saltmarrow-chronology]] starts here.

## Key Details

- Dates: 11 November 1918 to 1 September 1939.
- Electric lighting, cars and radio spread unevenly; the article's "common" describes the better-off.

## Caveats

A global overview. Nothing in it concerns any particular small town, and the novel never claims otherwise.

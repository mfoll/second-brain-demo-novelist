# Is the memorial chapter too sentimental?

## Question

Chapter 25 is the memorial service in December 1927, seen by Nell, with Constance conducting the choir. It is the emotional peak of the book. Does it tip into sentiment?

## Current Answer

Open. The draft 2 version (then chapter 37) was too much: hymn words, weeping widows, a child's hand. [[sunniva-brekke]]'s notes ([[sunniva-brekke-notes-on-memorial-chapter-2026-09]]) proposed eleven cuts of adjectives around the singing; I took nine. The third-draft version is not written yet.

## Working rules for the rewrite

- Nell is in the alto line, singing, so she cannot watch the widows. She sees the music and Constance's hands.
- Name one hymn, quote one line at most, and let the reader hear the parts rather than the words.
- No tears described in the choir.
- Constance's letter before it (chapter 24) carries the anger; chapter 25 carries only the sound.

## What would settle it

Reading it aloud at the writing group. If Sunniva and [[marcus-ellery]] both stay quiet at the end, it works.

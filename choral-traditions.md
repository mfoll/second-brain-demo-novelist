# Choral Traditions

Exploration cluster on chapel and community singing: hymns, four-part harmony, how an amateur choir is built and held together. It feeds [[constance-leal]]'s letters, but I sing for its own sake now.

## What lives here

- Background: [[hymn]] and [[four-part-harmony]].
- From inside: I joined [[ingrid-solheim]]'s community choir in September 2025 ([[joined-the-wednesday-choir-2025-09-10]]) and sang in the [[remembrance-concert-2025-11-09]]. Rehearsal notes in [[wednesday-choir-rehearsal-notes]].
- An object: [[chapel-hymnal-1926]], a second-hand hymnal with a previous owner's pencil marks.
- A craft idea that came from it: [[hymn-as-structure]].

## Status

Active while I keep singing. What it has given the book: Constance's altos, the sound of a choir learning a part line by line, and the knowledge that a memorial hymn is chosen by committee and argued over.

## What I have learned from inside

- A choir is built line by line. Constance's letters now show that work rather than the finished sound.
- The altos carry the harmony and get the least credit, which became a running joke in Constance's letters to her sister.
- Programmes are argued over. The memorial in December 1927 will be, too ([[which-hymn-does-the-choir-sing-at-the-memorial]]).
- Ingrid's answers on how a 1920s chapel choir might have been run are recorded with their limits in [[ingrid-solheim-choir-questions-2025-10]].

## Boundaries

The choir's members are not named, and nothing I hear in rehearsal about people's lives goes into the vault or the book.

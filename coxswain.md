# Coxswain

## Summary

A coxswain commands a boat and, above all, steers and navigates it. The article traces the word to the cockboat, a ship's boat, and "swain", servant, and gives an eighteenth-century definition of the officer in charge of a ship's boat and its crew.

## Why It Matters

[[tobias-arden]] is Saltmarrow's lifeboat coxswain. The point I took from the article is that the coxswain steers and commands; he does not row. In the storm chapters Tobias's hands are on the steering oar, and his job is to read the sea and the men, which is where his war and his nerve meet. Written into [[pulling-and-sailing-lifeboat]].

## Key Details

- In charge of navigation and steering.
- The title survived the obsolete cockboat.

## Caveats

The article is general and covers rowing coxswains and naval ranks too; lifeboat practice I checked against the station records.

# Tidewrack Festival Talk

My panel on translation and historical voice at the Tidewrack Book Festival, chaired by [[kwabena-osei|Kwabena Osei]], on 2026-06-20.

## Outcome

Panel delivered on 2026-06-20. Completed.

## Current State

- Delivered: 60 minutes, three panellists, about 140 in the tent. Account in [[tidewrack-festival-panel-2026-06-20]].
- Fee 250 GBP plus rail fare, paid 2026-07-17 ([[tidewrack-speaker-agreement-2026]]).

## Scope and Boundaries

The talk only. The festival's wider programme is not my business. I agreed in advance with Priyanka that The Salt Choir would be mentioned only as "the next one, set in the 1920s".

## Working Context

[[orla-fenwick|Orla Fenwick]] invited me on 2026-03-02 for a panel she was calling "Other people's voices": a translator-turned-novelist, a historical novelist and a poet who writes in dialect. The planning call on 2026-03-05 ([[tidewrack-programme-meeting-2026-03-05]]) fixed the format. Kwabena sent his questions on 2026-06-08 ([[tidewrack-panel-brief-2026]]); my answers became [[tidewrack-panel-notes]]. The question that mattered was whether translating teaches you to hear a period voice or to hide your own. I argued the first, using [[free-indirect-style]] as the bridge, and the panel came back to it twice.

# Oral History Interview With Dorothy Pengelly

On 2025-04-23 I interviewed [[dorothy-pengelly]], aged 94, the daughter of a herring gutter, about what her mother told her of the gutting work in the 1920s and what she herself remembered of the yards in the 1930s. About an hour and fifty minutes, recorded, at a place she chose. Introduction through [[ifeoma-clarke]] after the [[oral-history-workshop-2025-03-29|Merrin workshop]].

## Consent and governance

- Written consent signed at the start, terms read aloud first: [[dorothy-pengelly-consent-form]]. Background use for the novel; no direct quotation in the book without asking again; name in the acknowledgements only; a copy of the recording for her; deposit with the Merrin oral history collection if she chooses (she chose to in August 2025).
- The recording is a governed pointer, not transcribed in full: [[dorothy-pengelly-interview-recording]]. I made a timed summary log instead, which is what this note draws on.
- Method followed: [[run-an-oral-history-interview]].

## What she talked about

Summarised in my words; nothing here is a direct quotation.

- **The crew of three.** Two gutters and a packer, the same three women season after season, paid together by the barrel and splitting it. Her mother was a gutter for eleven seasons and then a packer, which was better paid and harder on the back.
- **Hands.** Cotton strips wound round the fingers against the knife and the salt, changed at midday. Cuts that went bad were the real danger. No gloves.
- **Speed and singing.** The crews sang to keep pace, hymns more than anything, and her mother said you could tell which yard was behind by which hymn it had reached. This went straight to [[hymn-as-structure]] and to Nell's chapter 1.
- **The season as the calendar.** Weddings, debts and quarrels arranged around it. The money arrived at the end and went quickly.
- **The lifeboat.** She remembered maroons going up at night in the 1930s and the whole town going down to the beach, women first. No specific loss; she was clear about that, and I have not attached anything she said to a real event.

## What I took and what I did not

- Took: the crew of three, the hands, the singing, the women going to the beach. All as texture, unattributed, changed in detail.
- Did not take: her mother's name, the names of the firms she worked for (she corrected two names by letter afterwards; I removed both because they were real), any family story she told after asking me to switch off the recorder.
- The line between texture and her story is the subject of [[how-much-of-dorothys-story-can-the-novel-use]], still open.

# Callum Prologue Call 2026-09-17

Monthly call with [[callum-pryce|Callum Pryce]], Thursday 17 September. Mostly the prologue; also the Ashlar enquiry, which had not yet become an offer, and the outline for the [[third-book-option]].

## The prologue

Callum had reread the current four-page [[storm-prologue]] with part one. His position, unchanged since July ([[callum-email-on-prologue-2026-07-10]]):

- Teodora reading "waiting for the boat" is exactly what he feared. The reader waits instead of living in 1924.
- A crime reader opens a book with a body and expects the book to explain it. A literary reader opens a book with a disaster and expects the book to arrive at it. Either way the ending is known.
- He would cut it and open on chapter 1 as written.

My answer: the reader knows a boat goes out; they do not know who, or whether it comes back, or what the town does after. That is the book's question. He said I was describing what I meant the prologue to do, not what it does.

## Outcome

No change. We agreed the evidence that would settle it: Teodora on part two, a cold reader on the whole draft ([[wendy-farrant]]), and Imogen's read. Callum asked that the decision be made before delivery and not left to copy-edit. Agreed. I drafted a fallback opening for chapter 2 on 2026-09-21 in case the prologue goes.

## Other business

- Book three: he repeated that he wants the two pages by the end of January 2027, when the third draft goes to Imogen ([[third-book-option]]).
- He had heard Ashlar Editions might approach me for a translation and asked me to say nothing until he had seen the terms ([[should-i-take-the-ashlar-translation-commission]]).

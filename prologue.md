# Prologue

Wikipedia article, English. Consulted 2026-07-06, two days before the [[draft-two-review-2026-07-08|draft two review]], when I was deciding whether to propose keeping a storm opening.

## Summary

Describes a prologue as an opening that sets context and gives background before the main story, often an earlier story tied to it, and traces the word to ancient Greek drama, where it had a broader sense closer to a preface.

## Why It Matters

Background for the four-page [[storm-prologue]] and the argument with Callum over it ([[should-the-salt-choir-keep-the-storm-prologue]]). Mine is not background in the article's sense: it is the central event shown early and cut off. That is closer to a flash-forward, which is part of why Callum objects.

## Caveats

General reference only. Says nothing about whether prologues help or hurt a novel; that is my own stance in [[a-prologue-must-earn-its-pages]].

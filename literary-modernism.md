# Literary modernism

## Summary

Modernist literature began in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and set itself apart from traditional ways of writing poetry and prose. It experimented with form and expression, aimed to overturn established modes of representation, and responded to the human cost of the First World War and to the technological and social change of the period.

## Why It Matters

Background for the [[modernist-women-writers]] cluster, read in May 2025 before rereading [[mrs-dalloway]]. The useful point for The Salt Choir is the link the article draws between the war's cost and the break in form: my characters live in the same years as the modernists but would never read them. The novel's form is conventional on purpose; its subject is the same broken decade.

## Key Details

- A self-conscious break with the past is the movement's common thread.
- The First World War pushed writers to reassess received assumptions.

## Caveats

A broad overview with a canon of mostly metropolitan writers; nothing to do with herring towns.

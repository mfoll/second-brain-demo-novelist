# Period Diction

Writing dialogue and close-third narration so that characters of the 1920s sound of their time without the prose becoming a costume drama.

## My rules

- **No modern concepts in the characters' heads.** The obvious ones (stress, trauma, closure) are easy; the hidden ones are attitudes. [[femi-adewale]] caught Tobias thinking about his "triggers" in all but name.
- **Period words only where they carry meaning.** "Wireless", "drifter", "the dole", "nerves" are in; decorative slang is out.
- **No phonetic dialect.** Local speech shows in word order and a few nouns, not apostrophes.
- **The narration may be slightly more modern than the dialogue**, as long as free indirect passages stay in the character's vocabulary ([[free-indirect-style]]).
- **Check dates of words** when in doubt, and log anything that slipped in [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]].

## Examples fixed in the third draft

- "Okay" removed from Nell's mouth (it had crept in twice in draft 2).
- Tobias's "flashbacks" became "when it comes on him".
- Constance's "boundaries" became nothing at all; she would not have had the word or needed it.

The [[back-translation-test]] helps: a word that is only a period flavour usually does not survive it.

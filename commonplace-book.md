# Commonplace book

## Summary

Commonplace books are personal notebooks for compiling whatever the owner finds interesting or useful: notes, proverbs, maxims, quotations, letters, poems, recipes, tables of measures, prayers and professional references. People have kept them since antiquity, and especially in the Renaissance and the nineteenth century.

## Why It Matters

Looked up in the first week of [[craft-notebook-digitisation]], when I realised my translation notebooks were commonplace books with a craft bias. It helped me decide what the vault is for: the vault is the index and the commonplace book's best entries, not a replacement for the notebook. Behind [[notebooks-beat-apps-for-first-capture]].

## Key Details

- The form is defined by the owner's interest, not by subject.
- Mixed contents (quotations, notes, practical references) are the norm.

## Caveats

History of a practice, not advice. Mine is a working notebook, less ordered than the classic form.

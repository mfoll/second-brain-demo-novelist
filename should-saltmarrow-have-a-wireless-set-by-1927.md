# Should Saltmarrow Have A Wireless Set By 1927?

Chapter 11 of the third draft has a wireless set at a chapel social in July 1926. Draft 2 had sets "in every front room". How common would a set have been in a poor herring town by the mid-1920s?

## Current Answer

> [!info]
> One set in town, at the Institute reading room, bought by subscription in 1926 and brought down to the chapel social for an evening. A handful of private sets among the better-off on the Brae by 1929. Not in every front room. Settled 2026-08-15 at Ferrow House and logged in the [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]].

## Reasoning

- [[history-of-radio]] gives the general picture: public broadcasting in the early 1920s, sets spreading through the decade, cost and reception limiting them at first. It says nothing about any town like Saltmarrow.
- A crystal set or a valve set with an aerial is a large purchase for a gutter's household; for the Institute, a committee's purchase is plausible and gives a scene.
- The scene works better with one set: everyone crowding round it, the minister uneasy about the music.

## Rejected

- No wireless at all until 1929. Too tidy, and it loses chapter 11's best scene.
- A set at the telegraph office. Nell's office is wires, not broadcasts; mixing them confused readers of draft 2.

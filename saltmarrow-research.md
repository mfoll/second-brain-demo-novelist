# Saltmarrow Period Research

The research behind The Salt Choir: what an invented herring port and chapel town would have looked, sounded and worked like between 1924 and 1929, checked chapter by chapter against the draft. Saltmarrow is invented, so the research is about plausibility, not about any real place.

## Outcome

A research dossier on 1920s coastal life checked against every chapter of the delivered manuscript, with the [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]] closed (no open entries) before delivery on 2027-03-31.

## Current State

- The [[saltmarrow-town-bible]] is stable: harbour, curing yards, telegraph office, lifeboat house, Bethel chapel, population about 4,000. Last structural change was 2026-05-14, when the lifeboat house lost its slipway winch engine along with the boat's engine.
- The lifeboat question is settled: ten-oar pulling and sailing boat, no engine, a motor boat promised for 1928. See [[would-saltmarrows-lifeboat-have-had-an-engine-in-1927]] and the [[merrin-archive-visit-2026-05-12]].
- The anachronism log has 41 entries, 34 fixed, 7 open. All seven open entries are in part two and three of the third draft, which is not yet rewritten.
- Part one of the third draft (1924 to 1926) has been checked once against the log using [[check-a-chapter-for-anachronisms]].
- Biggest remaining gap: how news of the 1929 crash reaches the town for the last chapters. See [[how-would-saltmarrow-hear-of-the-wall-street-crash]].

## Scope and Boundaries

Period facts, objects, work, speech and institutions; the archive and the oral history; the town's geography. Characters on the page and structure belong to [[salt-choir-third-draft]] and the [[character-bible]]. Real events are background only: nothing real happens in Saltmarrow, and no real town, region or country is named in the book or in these notes. Dorothy Pengelly's interview is used under the terms recorded in [[dorothy-pengelly-consent-form]]; the recording itself stays a pointer.

## Working Context

The research has three layers, and I keep them apart because they fail in different ways:

1. **Archive** at the [[merrin-maritime-collection]]: lifeboat station service records, fishing registers, curing-yard ledgers and photographs. Two visits so far, [[merrin-first-visit-2025-06-10]] and [[merrin-archive-visit-2026-05-12]]. [[eluned-parry]] is the archivist; [[ifeoma-clarke]] the curator.
2. **Memory**: the [[dorothy-pengelly-interview-2025-04-23|interview with Dorothy Pengelly]], whose mother gutted herring through the 1920s. It gave me the work's rhythm and its injuries, not facts I could check.
3. **Reading**: Wikipedia and general histories for background, logged as Sources with their limits, for example [[herring]], [[lifeboat-rescue]] and [[shell-shock]]. Background never becomes a claim about the town.

[[ronan-maddox]], a retired harbour pilot in the evening class, has turned into an unofficial maritime reader. His query about the engine came before the archive confirmed it. [[femi-adewale]] read the Tobias chapters for the shell shock material; that thread is tracked with the draft, not here.

The working rule from [[research-iceberg]] still holds: most of this never reaches the page, and the [[period-texture-vs-period-trivia]] test decides what does.

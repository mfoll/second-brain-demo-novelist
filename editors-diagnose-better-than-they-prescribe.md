# Editors Diagnose Better Than They Prescribe

## Stance

When an editor or a good reader says something is wrong, they are almost always right that something is wrong and often right about where. Their proposed fix is a suggestion. So is mine. The diagnosis deserves more time than the cure.

## Scope

Structural and character notes from [[imogen-lacey]], [[callum-pryce]], [[teodora-vlasic]] and the writing group. Less true of factual corrections, which are usually just right ([[femi-adewale]], [[ronan-maddox]]).

## Why I think this

- Every diagnosis of The Salt Choir has held up: the storm too late, Nell unable to carry the town, Tobias's war lost in the braid. My cure for the first two (the braid) was wrong in half.
- As a translator I saw the same with publishers' readers: right about the bad page, wrong about why.

## What would change my mind

A case where I acted on a diagnosis I disagreed with and the book got worse.

## Implications

Read notes, wait three days, write down in one sentence what I think the actual problem is, then reread the suggested fixes. Bring two or three options to the call, not one ([[editorial-call-2026-02-19]]). And when I propose the fix myself, test it against a cold read before committing five months to it.

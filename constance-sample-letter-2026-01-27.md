# Constance Sample Letter 2026-01-27

The letter I wrote alongside the class on [[class-session-2026-01-27|27 January 2026]], doing [[maureen-tighe]]'s exercise: a letter from a character to the one person she does not perform for. Constance Leal to her sister Agnes. About 600 words, handwritten in the class notebook, typed up the next morning.

## What It Is

Constance writing in spring 1924 about taking over the Bethel choir: the altos, the chapel committee, the herring fleet late in, and, never named, her husband. It was the first time she said what she thought. The two sentences I kept unchanged are quoted in [[constance-leal]]; nothing else from it is in the vault.

## What Became Of It

- Rewritten as the first of Constance's letters in draft 2 (about 1,400 words), and kept in the third draft.
- Its rule, that Constance only ever addresses Agnes, is now a consistency rule in [[constance-leal]].
- Used in class, anonymised as "a tutor's attempt", in the autumn 2026 session on point-of-view drift, as an example of a voice that stays put.

## Where It Lives

Notebook 12, pages 31 to 34; typed copy in the Scrivener project under Research / Class. The novel's letters themselves are tracked in [[letter-chapters]].

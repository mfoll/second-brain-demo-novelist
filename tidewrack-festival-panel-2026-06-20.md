# Tidewrack Festival Panel 2026-06-20

Panel "Other people's voices: translation and historical voice" at the Tidewrack Book Festival, Saturday 2026-06-20, 14:00 to 15:00 in the smaller tent. Chaired by [[kwabena-osei|Kwabena Osei]]. Panellists: me, a historical novelist on her fourth book, and a poet who writes in a coastal dialect.

## At a Glance

About 140 people, a full tent. Sixty minutes: ten-minute openings each, thirty minutes of chaired discussion, fifteen of audience questions (we overran by five). 38 copies of Lantern Hours signed at the festival shop afterwards.

## What I said

- My opening: twenty-two years of translating taught me to hear a voice before I could write one, and that the work of a historical novel is closer to translation than to research. You carry a voice across a distance without dressing it up. Notes in [[tidewrack-panel-notes]].
- I used [[free-indirect-style]] as the bridge: the translator's habit of letting the original's idiom colour the narration, the novelist's habit of letting a character's idiom do the same.
- On the period voice: the historical novelist argued for a light archaism; I argued against it, because readers in 1925 did not hear their own speech as old. The poet said dialect is neither archaic nor modern, only local, and that settled it better than either of us.
- On book two: only "the next one is set in a 1920s coastal town and has a choir in it". Agreed beforehand with Priyanka. This was a month before the [[long-margin-interview-2026-07-15|Long Margin misquote]], so it was luck as much as discipline.

## Questions from the floor

- Whether translators should be named on covers (yes; I said it before Kwabena could ask).
- Whether I ever wanted to "improve" an author I translated (often; I never did, as far as I know).
- A student from the [[evening-class-spring-2026|spring class]] asked about letters as a form. I said I was finding out.

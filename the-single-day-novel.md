# The Single-Day Novel

A novel, or a long section, confined to one day, with the past entering through memory. The model I know best is [[mrs-dalloway]], one June day in 1923.

## What it does

- Compression: every scene is under the pressure of the clock.
- Memory carries the backstory, so exposition becomes thought.
- The ending is fixed in advance (the day ends), which frees the writer to concentrate on how rather than what.

## Considered for The Salt Choir

In February 2026, while rebuilding draft 2, I considered telling the storm as a single day, 24 hours from the first gale warning, with 1924 to 1929 in memory. Rejected within a week: the book's three viewpoints would all have been remembering, and remembering is the slowest thing a novel does. The storm prologue is what is left of the idea.

## Use in class

Good for an exercise: write one hour of a character's day in which a past event comes back twice. Taught in the spring 2026 class on point of view.

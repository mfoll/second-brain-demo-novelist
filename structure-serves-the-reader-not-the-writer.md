# Structure Serves The Reader Not The Writer

## Stance

A novel's structure is good if a reader coming to it cold always knows where and when they are, and wants to go on. How elegant it is to plan, or how much it pleases the writer to have solved, does not count.

## Scope

Long fiction. Short stories can afford more puzzle.

## Why I think this

The braid of draft 2 was the most satisfying planning I have done: three strands, a frame, symmetries. It was hard to read. Imogen's colleagues lost Tobias's timeline; readers counted the crew from the storm fragments ([[draft-two-review-2026-07-08]]). I knew all the strands, so I could not see the problem; [[full-draft-read-through]] exists because of it.

## What would change my mind

A structure I find hard to plan that readers find easy, or the reverse turning out well. Also [[mrs-dalloway]], which I reread in March and which is both elegant and easy, so the conflict is not absolute.

## Implications

Judge structure by a cold read, mine after a week away or a first reader's. Prefer chronology unless displacement buys something specific, and name what it buys.

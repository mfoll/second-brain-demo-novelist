# Hymn As Structure

The idea that a hymn's shape (verses that repeat a tune with changing words, a refrain, four parts that only make sense together) can shape a sequence of chapters or a single chapter.

## Where it came from

Two sources a few months apart: Dorothy Pengelly's mother's remark, as Dorothy told it, that you could tell which yard was behind by which hymn it had reached ([[dorothy-pengelly-interview-2025-04-23]]); and learning an alto line at [[ingrid-solheim]]'s choir, where the line is dull alone and right in the chord ([[joined-the-wednesday-choir-2025-09-10]]).

## How it shows in The Salt Choir

- Three viewpoints as parts in one chord: Nell, Tobias and Constance each incomplete, the town audible only together. This is my private justification for keeping three viewpoints through the July 2026 reversal.
- Constance's nine letters work like verses: the same form, new words, the tune deepening.
- Chapter 1 is paced to a hymn the crew sings while gutting: three short movements and a pause.

## Limits

A metaphor for me, not a scheme for the reader. When I tried to make every part end on a hymn in draft 2, Imogen called it "organ music"; it went.

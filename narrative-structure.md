# Narrative Structure

Retrieval surface for how a novel is ordered: where it begins, what it withholds, how time runs, and what the reader is told first. For me this is mostly the long argument with The Salt Choir about where to put the storm.

## How it touches my work

The Salt Choir has had three structures in three drafts, and the reasons each one changed are in [[salt-choir-structure-history]]. Linear with one viewpoint ([[salt-choir-first-draft]]), a braid opening on the storm ([[braided-structure-rewrite]]), and chronological with a prologue ([[salt-choir-third-draft]]). Lantern Hours was simpler: one timeline, one week, with the dead mentor's translation as a frame.

## What I have learned so far

A structure that is interesting to build is not the same as one that is easy to read. The braid was the most satisfying planning I have ever done and the hardest book to follow. The reader needs to know where they are in time before they can care what happens next; cleverness about order spends that attention. A prologue is the one displacement I still think the book can afford, because it buys a question ("who will not come back?") for the cost of four pages. [[callum-pryce]] thinks it buys an answer instead. That disagreement is the live edge of this topic.

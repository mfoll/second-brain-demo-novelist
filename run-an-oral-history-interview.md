# Run An Oral History Interview

How I prepare, record and look after an oral history interview for a novel. Adapted from the [[oral-history-workshop-2025-03-29|Merrin workshop]] and used for [[dorothy-pengelly-interview-2025-04-23]].

## Before

1. Introduction through someone the interviewee trusts; never cold.
2. A short letter: who I am, that I am writing a novel (not a history), what I would like to talk about, how long, and that they can stop at any time.
3. A list of themes, not questions. For Dorothy: the work, the hands, the season, the town, the lifeboat.
4. The consent form prepared in large print ([[dorothy-pengelly-consent-form]] is the model): use as background, quotation only with fresh permission, how they are named, where the recording goes.
5. Test the recorder; spare batteries; a second recorder.

## During

1. Read the consent form aloud and sign it before recording.
2. Open questions, concrete prompts ("what did your mother's hands look like after a day?"), and silence.
3. If asked to stop the recorder, stop it, and do not write down what follows.
4. Stop at ninety minutes unless they want to go on. Dorothy wanted to go on.

## After

1. Timed summary log within 48 hours, in my words.
2. Thank-you letter with the summary within two weeks; invite corrections.
3. Store the recording as a governed file, not in the vault ([[dorothy-pengelly-interview-recording]]); copy to the interviewee and, if they choose, to the museum.
4. Record what I may and may not use in the interview's Event note.

## Guardrails

No family names, addresses or health details in the vault. Nothing said off the record is ever used. When in doubt, ask them, following [[shared-authority]].

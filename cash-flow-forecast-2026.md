# Cash-Flow Forecast 2026

My monthly cash-flow spreadsheet for the writing business, one row per month from January 2026 to June 2027. This note summarises the version of 16 October 2026, the one that shows the gap. Updated monthly by [[update-the-cash-flow-forecast]].

## Summary

When delivery of The Salt Choir moved from 2026-09-30 to 2027-03-31 ([[delivery-date-extension]]), the 8,000 GBP delivery tranche moved from a forecast November 2026 to a forecast May 2027. Between them sit six months, November 2026 to April 2027, with no advance and no large royalty payment. Without new income the account falls below my 2,000 GBP floor in January and is overdrawn from February, lowest at minus 3,145 GBP at the end of April 2027.

## Key Details

Base case, no grant, no commission. Outgoings 1,680 GBP a month (drawings 1,500, business costs 180), plus the January tax payment.

Scenarios on separate tabs:

- **Vellacott award** (6,000 GBP, paid early January if granted on 2026-12-11): April low point 2,855. Above the floor all the way.
- **Ashlar commission alone** (9,500 GBP, half on signature in December, half on delivery June 2027): April low point 1,605. Positive throughout, just under the floor in April.
- **Ashlar co-translated with Aurelio** (my half: 2,375 on signature): April low point -770.
- **Both award and commission**: comfortable, and no time to write.

## Why It Matters

This is the spreadsheet behind three live decisions: why the [[vellacott-grant-application]] went in, why [[neil-gathercole]] argues for the commission ([[neil-gathercole-cash-note-2026-10-14]]), and why [[should-i-take-the-ashlar-translation-commission]] is not simply a craft question. The gap is a timing gap, not a loss: the money is contracted ([[salt-choir-contract]]), it is just later.

## Caveats

- The May 2027 tranche assumes acceptance within a month of delivery on 31 March. If Imogen asks for revisions after delivery, it moves again ([[advances-arrive-late]]).
- Spring 2027 teaching is assumed but not yet confirmed by the centre.
- The PLR figure is last year's payment, rounded up a little for the paperback's library copies.
- Drawings are the business figure, not a household budget, which lives elsewhere.

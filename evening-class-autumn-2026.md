# Autumn 2026 Evening Class

Ten Tuesday sessions on revision, 15 September to 24 November 2026, for 14 students. The first term built around taking an existing draft apart rather than writing new pages.

## Outcome

Each student brings a draft of at least 3,000 words and leaves on 24 November with a revision plan and one fully revised section. Ten sessions delivered.

## Current State

- Six sessions done (15, 22 and 29 September; 6, 13 and 20 October). Half term on 27 October. Four left: 3, 10, 17 and 24 November.
- 14 students. Returning: [[ronan-maddox]], [[maureen-tighe]], [[lydia-chen-walsh]], [[samir-benhaddou]] and one other from spring. New: [[olu-bankole]] and eight others.
- The [[reverse-outline]] session ([[class-session-2026-10-06]]) worked best so far; three students rebuilt their plans from it.
- Fee 800 GBP, on the November timesheet, forecast for December in the [[cash-flow-forecast-2026]].

## Scope and Boundaries

Revision only, which needed a rule at enrolment: no one joins without an existing draft. Two applicants without one were pointed to Kiran's poetry group or the spring term. I am revising the third draft of The Salt Choir in the same weeks, which is useful and also a risk: I keep the class on their drafts and use mine only as a source of short examples of cuts, never as a workshop text. Student material follows [[student-writing-privacy-rules]].

## Plan

From [[autumn-2026-course-outline]]:

1. 15 September: what revision is and is not. Reading as a stranger.
2. 22 September: the one-sentence test for each chapter or section.
3. 29 September: cutting by ten per cent.
4. 6 October: [[reverse-outline]] ([[reverse-outline-handout-2026-10-06]]).
5. 13 October: point of view drift.
6. 20 October: openings revisited (using the autumn 2025 material).
7. 3 November: scene order and time.
8. 10 November: sentences, finally.
9. 17 November: feedback that a writer can use; peer pairs.
10. 24 November: reading and revision plans.

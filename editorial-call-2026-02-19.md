# Editorial Call 2026-02-19

Video call with [[imogen-lacey|Imogen Lacey]] to respond to her [[imogen-editorial-letter-2026-01-30|editorial letter of 30 January]] on the first draft of The Salt Choir. Thursday, 10:30 to 12:05. [[callum-pryce|Callum Pryce]] joined for the last twenty minutes. My notes written up the same afternoon; daily context in [[recaps/daily/2026/2026-02-19|the recap for the day]].

## At a Glance

> [!note]
> Decision: rebuild The Salt Choir as a nonlinear braid of three points of view (Nell, Tobias, Constance), opening on the night of the November 1927 storm and moving outward in time from it. Constance's chapters become letters. Rewrite to start 2026-02-23. Later partly reversed at the [[draft-two-review-2026-07-08|draft two review]].

## Context

Imogen's letter made two diagnoses: the storm arrives too late (chapter 31 of 41, about three quarters of the way in), and [[nell-garrick|Nell]] alone cannot carry the town. Callum ([[callum-notes-on-draft-one-2026-01-16]]) and [[teodora-vlasic|Teodora]] ([[teodora-reader-notes-draft-one-2026-01-20]]) had said the same in their own words. I agreed with the diagnosis before the call; the question was the cure.

## What I proposed

I came with three options written out, and argued for the third:

1. **Keep the linear order and move the storm earlier**, to the middle of the book, with 1928 and 1929 as a long second half. Imogen: fixes the timing but not the town; the second half becomes an aftermath novel.
2. **Linear, three viewpoints**: add Tobias and Constance in close third, rotate chapter by chapter. Imogen liked this best at first. I thought the storm would still arrive late, just with more people waiting for it.
3. **A braid opening on the storm.** The first chapter is the storm night in Nell's telegraph office. Then three strands, one per viewpoint, each starting in 1924 and moving forward, with storm-night sections recurring between them, until all three meet at the memorial in December 1927. The aftermath (1928 to 1929) is a short final part.

Imogen's question was what the reader is waiting for if they already know about the storm. My answer at the time: who is on the boat. Each strand would make the reader care about a different man in the crew. I believed it.

## Constance's letters

Separate from the structure, I told Imogen about the letter I had written in Constance's voice at the [[class-session-2026-01-27|evening class of 27 January]]. She asked to see it, read it during the call, and said Constance should only ever speak like that. Agreed: Constance's chapters become letters to her sister ([[letter-chapters]], [[constance-leal]]). This part survived everything after.

## Callum's part

Callum joined at 11:45. He liked the braid immediately; he said it made the book sound like something he could sell in one sentence. He asked whether the rewrite would hold the delivery date of 2026-09-30. I said yes. It did not ([[delivery-date-extension]]).

## Decisions

- Draft 2 will be a braid of three viewpoints, opening on the storm night ([[braided-structure-rewrite]]).
- Constance's chapters will be letters.
- Tobias's chapters will be in close third, like Nell's; [[femi-adewale]] to be asked to read them for the shell shock.
- Target for draft 2: the end of May. Imogen to read in June.

## What I would note now

Imogen asked the right question (what is the reader waiting for?) and I answered it with a planning answer, not a reading answer. The [[draft-two-review-2026-07-08|July review]] is the reading answer. See [[salt-choir-structure-history]] and [[suspense-versus-surprise]].

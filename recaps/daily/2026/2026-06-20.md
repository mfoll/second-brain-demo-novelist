# Saturday 20 June 2026

## The Tidewrack panel

The panel on translation and historical voice ran from 14:00 in the smaller tent, chaired by [[kwabena-osei|Kwabena Osei]], about 140 people. The full account is in [[tidewrack-festival-panel-2026-06-20|the panel note]]. My opening ran long, twelve minutes against eight planned. The real argument was about archaism: the historical novelist wants a light period flavour in dialogue; I think people in 1925 did not hear themselves as old. The poet's line that dialect is only local ended it better than either of us.

## Book two in one sentence

Asked about the next book, I said only that it is set in a 1920s coastal town and has a choir in it, as agreed with Priyanka. Nobody pressed.

## Signing and after

38 copies signed at the festival shop. Dinner with the other panellists; the historical novelist recommended a book on 1920s women's work in the fisheries which I passed to the research list for [[saltmarrow-research]]. [[orla-fenwick|Orla]] mentioned 2027; answer deferred to February.

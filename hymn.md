# Hymn

## Summary

A hymn is a song written for adoration or prayer, usually addressed to a deity or a revered figure. The article covers the word's Greek origin (a song of praise), hymnody and hymnists, hymnals as collections, and the fact that hymns may be sung with or without accompaniment.

## Why It Matters

Read after my first week in [[ingrid-solheim]]'s choir to put names to what I was singing. The article is general; what matters for Constance's choir is that a hymn is first the congregation's song, which is why her work from 1924 to 1927 is turning a congregation into a choir. The practical check on which hymns Saltmarrow could sing is the [[chapel-hymnal-1926]].

## Key Details

- Hymns may or may not be accompanied.
- Collections are hymnals; writing hymns is hymnody.

## Caveats

A general survey across faiths and centuries; nothing particular to small chapels in the 1920s.

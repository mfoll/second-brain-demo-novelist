# Ingrid Solheim On Running A Chapel Choir

[[ingrid-solheim]]'s answers, by email on 2025-10-19, to eight questions about how a small 1920s chapel choir might have been run. Summarised.

## Summary

- A leader would teach mostly by ear and by tonic sol-fa rather than staff notation, with a few readers in each part.
- Hymns for ordinary services chosen by the minister; for special occasions, argued over by the minister, the leader and the deacons.
- Four-part singing grows from a congregation's unison over years, not months; three years is believable for a determined leader.
- A harmonium is likely in a poor chapel; an organ is a fundraising campaign.
- Memorial programmes are always disputed.

## Why It Matters

Constance's choir goes from unison in 1924 to four parts by April 1927, which Ingrid thought about right. The harmonium went into the [[saltmarrow-town-bible]].

## Caveats

She was clear these are informed guesses from experience, not historical research. I treat them as plausibility, not fact.

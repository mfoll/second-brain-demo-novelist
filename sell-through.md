# Sell-Through

The copies of a book that shops actually sell to readers, as opposed to the copies the publisher ships to shops. Shipments minus returns minus stock still on shelves.

## Why the distinction matters

- Royalty statements count net shipments, with a reserve held against the returns that are still to come. A strong first period on a statement can shrink later if shops send stock back.
- Sales teams and booksellers watch sell-through, because it decides reorders and reprints.
- For a new paperback the gap can be large. Lantern Hours to 2026-08-31: about 7,900 shipped net, about 6,300 through tills ([[lantern-hours-paperback-sales-report-2026-09]]).

## How I use it

- I read the royalty statement for money ([[lantern-hours-royalty-statement-2026-10]]) and Martin's sell-through for the health of the book.
- If sell-through keeps pace with shipments over the autumn, the reserve on the statement to 2026-12-31 should mostly come back.

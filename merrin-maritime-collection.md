# Merrin Maritime Collection

The regional maritime museum and archive I use for the Saltmarrow research: lifeboat station records, fishing registers, curing-yard ledgers and photographs. This note records what it holds, how to use the reading room and what each visit gave me. The records are about other towns' stations and boats; Saltmarrow does not exist, so nothing in them is ever attributed to it.

## Summary

- **Lifeboat station records**: service record books, crew lists, launch returns and correspondence with the district inspectors, for eleven stations, roughly 1890 to 1960. Finding aid: [[merrin-lifeboat-station-records-finding-aid]].
- **Fishing registers**: vessel registers and harbour dues books.
- **Curing-yard ledgers**: two firms' wage and barrel books, 1919 to 1934.
- **Photographs**: about 4,000 prints and negatives, catalogued by place and decade.
- **Oral history**: a museum collection of interviews from the 1970s onwards, to which Dorothy Pengelly's interview was added with her consent ([[dorothy-pengelly-interview-recording]]).

## Why It Matters

The Merrin is where the research stops being reading and becomes evidence. Two visits so far:

1. [[merrin-first-visit-2025-06-10]]: photographs and curing-yard ledgers with [[ifeoma-clarke]]. Fixed the layout of the yards and the gutting crews' pay in the [[saltmarrow-town-bible]].
2. [[merrin-archive-visit-2026-05-12]]: lifeboat station records with [[eluned-parry]]. Settled [[would-saltmarrows-lifeboat-have-had-an-engine-in-1927]]: no engine, a ten-oar pulling and sailing boat, and a motor boat promised for 1928.

## Key Details

Reading-room rules, as given on the booking confirmation ([[merrin-reading-room-booking-2026-05]]):

- Open Tuesday to Thursday, 10:00 to 16:00; closed 12:30 to 13:15.
- Book by email at least a week ahead with item references; up to ten items a day.
- Pencils only. No bags at the table; lockers by the door.
- Photography without flash for private research, with a day permit (5 GBP). No reproduction without a licence from the museum.
- Gloves for photographs only, not for paper.
- Fragile items (some crew lists) are produced as copies.

## Caveats

What I copy is my own notes, not transcriptions; the records are about other stations and named men, and the novel never borrows a station's boat, crew or loss. Eluned stresses that small-station practice varied, so the archive gives plausibility, not a rule.

# Four-part harmony

## Summary

Four-part harmony is music written for four voices, or four instruments or a keyboard, in which each part can sound a different note of each chord.

## Why It Matters

Constance's ambition for the Bethel choir: from a congregation singing the tune in unison in 1924 to four parts by April 1927 (third draft chapter 15). Read a week after my first rehearsal with [[ingrid-solheim]]'s choir, when I had just learned how dull an alto line is on its own. It is also the image behind [[hymn-as-structure]]: three viewpoints as parts of one chord.

## Key Details

- Four independent parts, each able to carry a different chord note.

## Caveats

A definition, not a history of chapel singing; Ingrid's answers fill that gap with stated limits.

# Unreliable narrator

Wikipedia article, English. Consulted 2026-03-12 while planning Constance's letters for draft 2 and deciding whether her sister should ever reply.

## Summary

Describes the unreliable narrator as one whose credibility is compromised, notes that the device is found in narrators of every age, and that such narrators are nearly always first-person, though some critics argue for unreliable second- and third-person narration, mostly in film and television.

## Why It Matters

Constance is the only first-person voice in The Salt Choir, and her letters report events late and selectively. She is not unreliable in the strong sense of the article; she is partial, which is the milder cousin. Reading this settled two things: no replies from Agnes on the page, so nothing corrects her ([[should-constances-sister-write-back]]), and her storm letter may get small facts wrong as long as the reader can see it against chapters 21 to 23 ([[storm-night-timeline]]).

## Caveats

The article's examples lean towards deliberate deceivers and mad narrators. Constance is neither.

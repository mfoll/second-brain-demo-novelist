# Saltmarrow Town Bible

The invented town of The Salt Choir, fixed so that the draft stops moving the chapel. Saltmarrow is a herring port and chapel town on an unnamed northern coast, 1924 to 1929. It exists only in the novel: no real town, county or country is named, and when I borrow a detail from background reading I change it until it could be anywhere on a cold coast.

> [!info]
> Population about 4,000 in the 1920s, swelling by several hundred in the herring season when the gutting crews and the curers' men come in. One harbour, three curing yards, one telegraph office in the post office, one lifeboat house, one parish church and the Bethel chapel, which is the bigger congregation.

## Geography

The town sits in a bay with the harbour at its east end and a shingle beach to the west. Streets climb from the water in three bands:

- **The Strand**: the harbour front. Fish quay, the three curing yards (Rendell's, the biggest; Hallett's; and the Co-operative yard), the cooperage, the ice house, the harbour master's hut.
- **Fore Street**: the shopping street, parallel to the shore. The post office and telegraph office (Walter Pascoe's), the bank, two pubs (the Anchor and the Crown), the Institute with its reading room, the doctor's house.
- **The Brae**: the steep upper streets. Bethel chapel at the top of Chapel Brae, the parish church of St Anne further west on its own green, the school, the better houses, Constance Leal's rented cottage.

The **lifeboat house** stands alone at the west end of the Strand, at the head of a slipway onto the beach, because the harbour mouth faces the wrong way in a north-easterly. That single fact makes the November 1927 launch harder and slower, and it is used in chapter 21.

The **bar**: a sandbank across the bay's mouth that breaks in heavy weather. Boats come home over it on the tide. The drifter in the storm is on the bar.

## Work

- Herring season from late spring to autumn; the fleet follows the shoals and comes and goes. Gutting crews of three (two gutters, one packer), mostly women. Pay by the barrel, with a small engagement payment at the start of the season. Detail from [[herring]], [[cooper-profession]] and the [[dorothy-pengelly-interview-2025-04-23|Dorothy Pengelly interview]].
- In winter the town shrinks to line fishing, repairs, and debt.

# Advance Against Royalties

English Wikipedia article, last edited 2020-05-17, read on 2025-03-10 while the Salt Choir contract was being finalised.

## Summary

A short article on the licensing term in general: an advance is paid by the licensee at the start of a licence and later offset against the royalties that the licence earns. It is also called a guaranteed minimum royalty, which is the clearer name: the author keeps it whether or not the book earns it back.

## Why It Matters

Before signing the [[salt-choir-contract]] I wanted the general mechanics straight rather than only Callum's version. Two points I took from it: an advance is not a fee on top of royalties, it is royalties paid early; and it is not returned if unearned. That framed how I read the 24,000 GBP in thirds, and later how I understood the Lantern Hours earn-out ([[lantern-hours-earn-out-check]]).

## Caveats

The article is generic and short, with no publishing-specific detail on tranches, reserves or agency commission. Those come from my own contracts and from Bethan Okafor.

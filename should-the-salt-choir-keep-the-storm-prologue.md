# Should The Salt Choir keep the storm prologue?

## Question

The third draft opens with a four-page prologue: the lifeboat launching into the November 1927 storm, no names, stopping as the boat clears the harbour ([[storm-prologue]]). Does it give the reader a question that drives parts one and two, or does it give the ending away?

## Current Answer

> [!info]
> Provisional: keep it, under the rules agreed with Imogen on 2026-07-08 (four pages, no names, no outcome). Not settled. Callum Pryce dissents and has not moved. The decision waits for first readers of parts one and two, and is made with Imogen before the third draft goes to her on 2027-01-29.

## The two positions

**Keep (me, [[imogen-lacey|Imogen]]):**

- The book's central event comes at chapter 21 of 33. Without the prologue, nothing in part one tells the reader the sea matters more than the yard and the office.
- The prologue asks "who will not come back?" and gives every crew scene a second weight ([[dramatic-irony]]).
- It names nobody and shows no outcome, which is the difference from the braid, where the storm returned in pieces until readers could list the dead.

**Cut ([[callum-pryce|Callum]], [[marcus-ellery|Marcus]]):**

- Any storm prologue tells the reader the lifeboat is lost; nobody writes a prologue about a rescue that works.
- A reader who knows that reads parts one and two as waiting, and literary readers will forgive a slow start more easily than a known ending.
- Chronological order with a prologue keeps the braid's spoiler without the design that justified it ([[callum-email-on-prologue-2026-07-10]]). Restated on the [[callum-prologue-call-2026-09-17|call of 17 September]] and in a text on 2026-10-20.

## Evidence so far

- [[teodora-vlasic]] read part one with the prologue and said she read it "waiting for the boat" ([[teodora-reads-part-one-2026-09-09]]). She meant it as praise. Callum read it as proof.
- The writing group, who know the book, are split two to two.
- Imogen's July view: Callum's worry is "the right worry", answered by what the prologue withholds.

## What would settle it

1. Teodora's read of part two (due early January 2027): does the waiting stay tension, or turn into impatience?
2. One reader who has not been told anything about the book reading parts one and two. [[wendy-farrant]] has agreed, for the whole third draft.
3. Imogen's read of the full third draft.

## Fallback if it goes

Move the maroons and the launch into chapter 2 as Tobias's first service launch in August 1924, so part one still opens to the sea. Drafted as a contingency on 2026-09-21; about 600 words.

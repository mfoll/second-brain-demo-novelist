# Long Margin Interview Published 2026-07-15

The Long Margin issue 31 came out on 2026-07-15 with my interview with [[jasper-meade|Jasper Meade]]: eight pages, recorded on 2026-06-03 ([[long-margin-interview-recording-2026-06-03]]). Most of it was good. The last column was not.

## What happened

- The interview ran under the heading "The translator's own book". Six of the eight pages are on translation, Lantern Hours and coming to fiction at 54, and they are accurate.
- In the final column the printed text described The Salt Choir as "a shipwreck novel" and gave its working title, before Larkspur & Venn had announced the book or its title.
- Priyanka saw it at 09:40 and phoned at 09:45. Callum emailed at 10:30, less calmly.

## What was wrong

> [!warning]
> Two errors: "shipwreck novel" was Jasper's gloss, not my phrase, and misdescribes a book about a town, a choir and three people of whom only one goes to sea; and the working title was printed before the publisher's announcement, which is the publisher's to make.

- "Shipwreck" is wrong in fact as well as tone. The lifeboat in the novel is lost on a rescue; it is not a shipwreck story and the storm is one night in five years of the town's life ([[salt-choir-structure-history]]).
- The title matters commercially. Larkspur & Venn announce titles in the catalogue, and [[final-title-decision]] is still open; a printed working title can stick.
- My share of the fault: I said the title after asking whether we were still recording, did not hear the answer clearly, and did not ask to check quotes, which the magazine allows.

## What followed

- 2026-07-17: call with [[priyanka-desai|Priyanka]] and [[callum-pryce|Callum]] ([[long-margin-correction-call-2026-07-17]]). Priyanka sent the correction request that afternoon.
- 2026-07-22: The Long Margin published an online correction removing "shipwreck novel", noting the title was provisional, and stating that the description was the interviewer's ([[long-margin-correction-notice-2026-07-22]]). The print issue stands.
- Since then: no public detail on book two beyond "a 1920s coastal town" until the catalogue announcement ([[what-i-say-in-public-about-book-two]]). Interviews by email where possible ([[email-interviews-over-recorded-ones]]).

## Effect

Small, as far as anyone can tell. Two booksellers asked me about "the shipwreck book" at readings in September; one reader's blog repeated the title. Imogen was unbothered about the title and more bothered about "shipwreck", which she thinks would sell the wrong book to the wrong readers.

# Letter Chapters

Whole chapters written as a character's letters, set inside a novel otherwise told in third person. A partial [[epistolary-novel]]: one correspondent, one direction, embedded in narration.

## Why it works for Constance

- It gives [[constance-leal|Constance]] the voice she uses only with her sister, which is her real one. In close third she was flat; in letters she is the sharpest mind in the book.
- It gives the reader a different contract: first person, chosen, partial. She tells Agnes what she wants Agnes to know.
- It lets the book report events after they happen, with a few days' perspective, so her storm letter does not repeat the storm chapters.
- Structurally it marks the rhythm: a letter closes each run of chapters, like a cadence.

## Rules I keep

1. One correspondent only, and no replies on the page ([[should-constances-sister-write-back]]).
2. Every letter must do something a scene could not: a judgement, a delay, a secret, a sum of money.
3. Dated and placed. The reader should always know when she is writing and how long after the event.
4. Period letter conventions, lightly: salutation, sign-off, a postscript where she says the true thing. No pastiche spelling.
5. Read aloud before finishing ([[sunniva-brekke]]'s habit).

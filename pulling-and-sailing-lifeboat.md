# Pulling And Sailing Lifeboat

The term for an open rescue boat driven by oars ("pulling") and by sail where the wind allowed, launched from a boathouse or beach and crewed by local volunteers. The standard type before motor lifeboats replaced them station by station through the 1920s and 1930s. Saltmarrow's boat, the *Providence*, is one.

## What it means for the book

- **Crew**: thirteen for a ten-oar boat, the oarsmen plus coxswain, second coxswain and bowman. See [[lifeboat-crew-roll]].
- **Launch**: down the slipway by hand and horse team; forty minutes from maroon to afloat in the storm.
- **Effort**: the crew are tired before they reach the casualty. The capsize comes on the return.
- **Command**: the coxswain steers with the steering oar or rudder and reads the sea; he does not row. [[tobias-arden]]'s hands are free, which matters for his chapters.
- **Words**: oars, thwarts, the drogue, sail set or not. Never "engine", "throttle" or "wheel".

# Stream of consciousness

## Summary

In literary criticism, stream of consciousness is a narrative method that tries to show the flow of thoughts and feelings through a mind, usually as interior monologue, often disjointed or irregularly punctuated. The article names modernist writers, Proust, Joyce, Dorothy Richardson and Woolf among them, as the ones who fully developed it in the twentieth century.

## Why It Matters

A boundary for The Salt Choir. I use [[free-indirect-style]] for Nell and Tobias, not stream of consciousness: the narration stays grammatical and close, and the reader is never inside an unpunctuated mind. Tobias's two war scenes came nearest, and I pulled them back. Useful in class for showing the difference.

## Key Details

- Interior monologue, often disjointed.
- Developed by modernist writers including two of the women in my reading cluster.

## Caveats

The terms overlap in criticism; my distinction is a working one.

# Spring 2026 Evening Class

Ten Tuesday sessions on voice and point of view, 13 January to 31 March 2026, for 14 students. The term that gave The Salt Choir its letters.

## Outcome

Ten sessions delivered, each student writing the same short scene in three points of view and one voice exercise of their choosing. Reached on 2026-03-31.

## Current State

- Completed. Fee 800 GBP, invoiced on the March timesheet and paid 2026-04-17 ([[carrowmede-timesheets-2026]]).
- Feedback forms: 12 of 14 returned; summary in [[spring-2026-class-feedback-summary]]. The epistolary session and the free indirect session were the most often named.
- Its two consequences for my own work are recorded elsewhere: Constance's chapters as letters ([[constance-leal]], from [[class-session-2026-01-27]]) and the engine on the lifeboat ([[spring-class-final-reading-2026-03-31]], later settled in [[would-saltmarrows-lifeboat-have-had-an-engine-in-1927]]).

## Scope and Boundaries

Voice and point of view only. Fourteen students, the centre's maximum: [[ronan-maddox]], [[maureen-tighe]], [[lydia-chen-walsh]] and [[agnieszka-wrona]] among them. Student writing is recorded only under [[student-writing-privacy-rules]]. I wrote alongside the class in three sessions, which I had not done in autumn; see [[teaching-feeds-the-writing]].

## Plan As Run

From [[spring-2026-course-outline]]:

1. 13 January: first person and the problem of the narrator who knows too much.
2. 20 January: close third and [[free-indirect-style]].
3. 27 January: epistolary voice. [[epistolary-voice-handout-2026-01-27]], Maureen's exercise ([[maureen-tighe-letter-exercise]]), my sample letter in Constance's voice ([[constance-sample-letter-2026-01-27]]).
4. 3 February: second person, mostly to show why it is rarely used.
5. 10 February: the unreliable narrator.
6. 24 February: dialect and period voice without phonetic spelling.
7. 3 March: distance, zooming in and out within a paragraph.
8. 10 March: one scene in three viewpoints (the term's main exercise).
9. 24 March: revision for voice consistency.
10. 31 March: reading.

Half term took 17 February; the centre was closed 17 March for a building inspection.

## What Worked

- Writing alongside the students. They trusted the exercises more once they had seen me struggle with one in the room.
- Maureen's exercise (a letter to the one person you do not perform for) was better than mine; I asked her if I could use it in later terms and credit it, and she agreed.
- Agnieszka, a poet moving into prose, had the best ear in the room for rhythm and the most trouble with plot, which she said was the whole reason she came.

## What Did Not

- Second person was a wasted evening. Replace it with frame narration next time.
- Fourteen is too many for a reading at the end. Seven minutes each, and still it ran to 21:20.

# What I Say In Public About Book Two

My rule for interviews, readings, panels and social posts, adopted on 2026-07-17 after [[long-margin-interview-2026-07-15|The Long Margin printed "a shipwreck novel" and the working title]].

## The rule

> [!note]
> Until Larkspur & Venn announce The Salt Choir in their catalogue, the whole public description is "a novel set in a 1920s coastal town". No title, no storm, no lifeboat, no choir, no character names.

## What I may say

- "It's set in the 1920s, in a coastal town that doesn't exist."
- "It's taking longer than I hoped." (True and harmless.)
- Anything about Lantern Hours, translation, craft or teaching.

## What I don't

- The working title, even as "provisional". Titles stick, and [[final-title-decision]] is open.
- The storm or the lifeboat. They make it sound like a disaster novel, which is how "shipwreck" happened.
- Structure, drafts or delays. The [[delivery-date-extension]] is between me, Callum and Imogen.
- Characters, including Constance's letters. The class knows about the letters; that stays in the room.

## Why

- The title and the description are the publisher's to announce, through the [[catalogue-announcement]].
- Interviews compress. Whatever I say about a book I am still writing will be shorter in print and wrong.
- I had already kept to a version of this at the [[tidewrack-festival-panel-2026-06-20|Tidewrack panel]]. It worked there because I had prepared it, not because I had a rule.

## When it ends

When the catalogue copy is out, probably in 2027. Priyanka will tell me the date. Then the catalogue copy is the description.

## Applied so far

- Driftline reading, 2026-09-24: two questions about "the shipwreck book", answered with the one sentence.
- Writing group and class: unaffected; they are not public.

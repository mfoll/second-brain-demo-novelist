# Imogen Editorial Letter 2026-01-30

[[imogen-lacey|Imogen Lacey]]'s structural letter on the first draft of The Salt Choir, eight pages, emailed on Friday 30 January 2026. Summarised here; the PDF is in the editorial folder.

## Summary

> [!abstract]
> Two diagnoses: the storm arrives too late (chapter 31 of 41), and Nell alone cannot carry the town. Imogen admires the prose, the curing yards and the telegraph office, and offers three possible directions without choosing one.

## Key details

- **Timing of the storm.** At about 74% of the way through, the book's central event is "a climax that the reader has to take on trust for 280 pages". Parts of 1925 and 1926 feel like waiting.
- **One viewpoint.** Nell is vivid but has to be everywhere; the lifeboat crew, the chapel and the committee are seen through doors. Imogen lists eight scenes where Nell's presence is "a convenience".
- **Constance.** "Interesting at a distance, absent close up." Imogen wants more of her or less.
- **Tobias.** The most interesting man in the book, seen only from outside.
- **Aftermath.** 1928 to 1929 reads as an epilogue at 19,000 words: too long for that, too short for a part.
- **Directions offered:** move the storm to the middle; add viewpoints in a linear structure; or "something bolder with time". She explicitly does not choose.
- **Praise:** the telegraph chapters, the gutting scenes, the hymn rehearsals, the sentences.

## Why it matters

The letter that started the rewrite. Its two diagnoses held up across both later drafts; my answer on the [[editorial-call-2026-02-19|editorial call]] (the braid, her third direction) was half right. See [[salt-choir-structure-history]] and [[editors-diagnose-better-than-they-prescribe]].

## Caveats

Imogen wrote "something bolder with time" as the third option, and I heard it as encouragement. Rereading in July, it was one option among three.

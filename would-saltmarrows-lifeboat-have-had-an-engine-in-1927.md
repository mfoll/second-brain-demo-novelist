# Would Saltmarrow's Lifeboat Have Had An Engine In 1927?

The storm chapters of draft 2 had the Saltmarrow lifeboat start its engine and push out through the surf. Was that plausible for a small station in November 1927?

## Current Answer

> [!success]
> No. Saltmarrow's boat, the *Providence*, is a ten-oar pulling and sailing lifeboat: rowed out through the surf, sailed where the wind allowed, launched by hand and horse team down the beach slipway. A motor lifeboat is promised by the district committee for 1928 and arrives in September 1928, after the loss, which turns the engine into a thread of the aftermath instead of a prop in the storm. Settled on 2026-05-14.

## Evidence

1. **The archive.** At the [[merrin-archive-visit-2026-05-12|Merrin Maritime Collection on 12 May 2026]], [[eluned-parry]] showed me the service records of three small stations. All three were still launching pulling and sailing boats in the late 1920s. Motor boats were arriving station by station through the decade as money and slipways allowed, bigger and busier stations first. A district inspector's letter in the same box recommended a motor boat for one station "when funds permit". References in [[merrin-lifeboat-station-records-finding-aid]] and [[eluned-parry-email-2026-05-15]].
2. **A reader who knows boats.** [[ronan-maddox]], a retired harbour pilot in the Tuesday class, asked who had started the engine after I read a page of the storm opening at the [[spring-class-final-reading-2026-03-31|spring class reading of 31 March 2026]]. He thought a small station would still have had a pulling and sailing boat. I noted it and did not act until the archive confirmed it.
3. **Background reading.** [[lifeboat-rescue]] and [[coxswain]] for the general history of rowed and motor lifeboats. Background only: none of it is about Saltmarrow, which does not exist.

## What changed in the book

- The storm sequence (third draft chapters 21 to 23) is being rewritten around oars: the launch takes longer, the crew are exhausted before they reach the drifter on the bar, and the capsize on the return comes from a following sea, not a stalled engine.
- The lifeboat house lost its winch engine ([[saltmarrow-town-bible]]).
- Crew fixed at thirteen, five lost: [[lifeboat-crew-roll]].
- The promised motor boat becomes part of [[tobias-arden]]'s part three: the boat he will not command.
- Logged as the 2026-05-14 entry in the [[saltmarrow-anachronism-log]].

## What I rejected

- Keeping the engine and adding an author's note. A reader who knows boats would stop trusting the whole storm.
- Making Saltmarrow a big station so that an engine was plausible. The town is small on purpose; a big station would need a bigger town.
- Moving the storm to 1931. The book's chronology and the 1929 ending depend on 1927.

## Caveats

The records show what three real stations had. Saltmarrow is invented, so the answer is about plausibility for a station of its size, not a fact about any particular place. Eluned was explicit that practice varied.

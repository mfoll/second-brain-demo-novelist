# Translation Only Between Books

## Stance

I take translation work only between novels, never while drafting one. A book of someone else's sentences takes over the part of my head that a draft needs, and it pays too well per week to stay small.

## Scope

Full-length literary translation. Not a sample chapter, a reader's report or checking a translator's French for a friend, all of which take a day and leave nothing behind.

## Why I Think This

- I wrote nothing of my own in the eighteen months of co-translating with [[aurelio-santangelo]] in 2016 and 2017.
- Lantern Hours started properly only when the last translation contracts ran out.
- The money is real and immediate in a way novel money is not, which makes it hard to stop once started ([[advance-tranches]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

- A co-translation where my share is small and comes after delivery, as in option 3 of [[ashlar-commission-options]]. That is the case now being tested, hence `Revising`.
- Evidence that a few weeks of translation sharpen rather than flatten my prose. I have none either way ([[does-translating-again-damage-my-own-voice]]).
- A cash gap with no other answer, which is close to the current position.

## Implications

- Default answer to translation offers while drafting: no.
- If I break the rule for Ashlar, the translating weeks go after delivery of The Salt Choir, not before.

# Reserve Against Returns

Bookshops can return unsold copies to the publisher for credit. Because a sale on a royalty statement may later be undone by a return, publishers hold back part of the royalties earned, a reserve against returns, and release it on later statements once returns are known.

## As It Applies To Me

- Held mostly on paperback royalties in the first period after publication, when the initial shipment is large and returns are unknown.
- Lantern Hours, statement to 2026-06-30: 781.99 GBP, 20% of the period's paperback royalties ([[lantern-hours-royalty-statement-2026-10]]).
- Larkspur & Venn's practice, per [[bethan-okafor]]: 20%, then 10%, then released.
- The Lantern Hours contract allows a "reasonable reserve" with no cap; the Salt Choir contract caps it at 25%.

## Planning Rule

A reserve is money owed, not money lost, but it is not cash until a statement releases it. It never goes into the forecast in advance ([[advances-arrive-late]]).
